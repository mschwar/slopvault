# The Civilizational Substrate Project

## Genesis
This project was sparked by a singular realization from *Chip War*: **Most of the world's GDP requires or relies on semiconductors—a product that did not exist 75 years ago.** 

How did a technology go from non-existent to an existential dependency for global civilization in a single human lifetime? 

This repository maps the acceleration, adoption, and scaling of core "civilizational substrates" in the United States from 1800 to the present. We are not tracking consumer fads; we are tracking the fundamental technologies that function as the metabolism, vascular system, and nervous system of the modern economy.

## Core Theses
1. **The Biological Super-Organism:** Since the Industrial Revolution, human civilization has built a planetary super-organism. It has a metabolism (energy/materials), a vascular system (logistics/transport), a nervous system (communications/compute), and an immune system (public health).
2. **The Law of Stacking Vulnerabilities:** Substrate technologies do not replace each other; they layer. The internet layers on telecom, which layers on electricity. As the stack grows taller, systemic efficiency approaches infinity, but systemic resilience approaches zero.
3. **Hard vs. Soft Substrates:** Adoption speeds are accelerating because early substrates (rail, electricity, telecom) were "Hard Substrates" requiring physical trenching. Modern substrates (software, AI) are "Soft Substrates" that piggyback on existing infrastructure, enabling near-instant diffusion.
4. **Exponential Scaling (Kurzweil/SFI):** The true impact of a substrate is not just its diffusion S-curve (how many people have it), but its fundamental scaling metric (how much cheaper/denser it becomes over time).

## Scope & Constraints
* **Geography:** United States focus (for data availability and consistency).
* **Timeline:** Strictly post-1800. We are tracking the modern industrial and information acceleration. 
* **Focus:** General-purpose technologies, enabling infrastructures, coordination systems, and physical materials.

# Master Database Schema

This defines the data structure for the `substrates_master.csv` file. The database is populated in two phases: Phase 1 (Baselines & Scaling) and Phase 2 (Diffusion Milestones).

### Core Identifiers
* **Domain:** `Metabolism` (Energy/Materials), `Vascular` (Transport/Logistics), `Nervous_System` (Compute/Comms), `Immune_System` (Health/Capacity).
* **Subdomain:** Specific categorical grouping (e.g., *Power, Materials, Wireless, AI*).
* **Technology:** The substrate name.
* **Substrate Type:** `Hard` (Requires new physical deployment) or `Soft` (Piggybacks on existing networks).
* **Primary Dependencies:** Comma-separated list of prior technologies this substrate requires to function (e.g., *Cloud Computing requires: Internet, Fiber Optics, Semiconductors, Electricity*).

### Timeline & Latency
* **Invention Year:** First workable/practical invention (Post-1800).
* **U.S. Commercial Launch (T0):** First meaningful U.S. commercial or public availability.
* **Delta (Years):** `T0 - Invention Year`.

### Metrics & Scaling
* **Fundamental Scaling Metric:** The unit of exponential cost/performance improvement (e.g., *Calculations per second per $, Cost per kWh, Cost per ton-mile*).
* **Recommended U.S. Adoption Metric:** How we measure its spread (e.g., *Household penetration, GDP exposure, Freight ton-miles*).
* **Denominator:** `Households`, `Population`, `Firms`, `GDP`, `Volume`.

### Diffusion Milestones (Phase 2)
* **T10:** Years from T0 to 10% adoption.
* **T25:** Years from T0 to 25% adoption.
* **T50:** Years from T0 to 50% adoption.
* **T75:** Years from T0 to 75% adoption.
* **Peak Adoption / Current Status:** Maximum penetration reached.

# Research Standards & Adjudication Rules

To prevent AI hallucination and ensure rigorous historical data, all agents must adhere to the following standards:

## 1. Defining "Invention" vs. "Commercial Launch (T0)"
* **Invention:** Must be a *working prototype or practical demonstration*, not merely a theoretical paper or scientific discovery. (e.g., Nuclear power invention is the first artificial fission reactor, not Einstein's E=mc^2).
* **T0 (Commercial Launch):** Must be the year ordinary citizens, firms, or municipalities in the U.S. could *purchase or utilize* the technology. A lab demo does not count. 

## 2. Direct vs. Indirect Adoption
If a technology is a component (e.g., Semiconductors, Electric Grid, Portland Cement), do not track direct consumer ownership. 
* *Incorrect:* "Consumers who own a semiconductor."
* *Correct:* "U.S. GDP exposure to semiconductor-dependent sectors" OR "Input-Output capital stock of IT equipment."

## 3. Sourcing Hierarchy
All date and metric claims must prioritize:
1. U.S. Statistical Abstract (Historical statistics).
2. Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) Input-Output tables.
3. Authoritative institutional histories (e.g., IEEE, DOE, Santa Fe Institute, NBER).

## 4. Dispute Logging
If historical consensus is split (e.g., "Who invented the radio?"), agents must log both dates in their scratchpad, select the most defensible U.S.-relevant date, and write a 1-sentence justification.
Agent Instructions (The First 3 Agents)
To kick off the pipeline, you will initialize three distinct agents.
