I like this a lot.

The instinct is right: **AI creation has exploded, but AI-native curation, identity, memory, and discovery have not.** Right now people can generate endlessly, but they do not have a satisfying place to **keep, organize, revisit, exhibit, and socially circulate** what they made.

The interesting part is that this is **not just “social media for AI.”** It is closer to:

**a personal creative vault + public portfolio + discovery network for AI-native artifacts.**

That is much stronger.

## The core problem

People using AI tools have a few recurring pains:

**1. Creation is easy; retrieval is terrible.**  
They make 50 good things and 5,000 forgettable things. The good things vanish into downloads folders, chat histories, scattered platforms, or cloud drives.

**2. There is no canonical home for AI-made work.**  
The artifact might begin in ChatGPT, Midjourney, Suno, Claude, Runway, NotebookLM, Cursor, etc. But none of those is really the enduring public shelf.

**3. Existing platforms do not fit the object.**

- Instagram/X/TikTok are feed-first, not archive-first.
    
- GitHub is amazing for code, bad for broad creative artifacts.
    
- Google Drive/Dropbox are storage, not showcase/discovery.
    
- DeviantArt/ArtStation fit some visual work, not multi-format AI artifacts.
    
- Reddit is discussion-first, not portfolio-first.
    
- ArXiv is credibility-first, not playful/public/iterative.
    

**4. AI artifacts are process-rich, but the process gets lost.**  
Prompt, model, edits, iterations, versions, collaborators, seeds, references, remixes—this is often as interesting as the final output.

That last point is important. The product may win not merely because it stores outputs, but because it stores **lineage**.

Your references are converging on a few product primitives:

The key question is: **what is the wedge?**

Because “a place for all AI things” is too broad for MVP.

## The right wedge

I would not start with “Reddit + DeviantArt for AI slop.”

That is catchy internally, but strategically dangerous:

- “slop” is funny, but it frames the space as low-value
    
- “everything AI-made” is too broad
    
- broad artifact types create impossible moderation, taxonomy, and UX problems
    

I would instead define the wedge as:

**A home for AI-native creations that people want to save, organize, and publicly display.**

Or even tighter:

**A profile-first gallery for AI-made artifacts with provenance, collections, and discovery.**

That gives you a much cleaner MVP.

## What the MVP actually is

At MVP, this should probably be:

### 1. Personal artifact pages

Each thing gets a durable page:

- title
    
- artifact type
    
- cover/preview
    
- description
    
- tags
    
- creation date
    
- tool/model used
    
- optional prompt/process
    
- optional files
    
- public/private/unlisted
    

This alone solves the “I made something cool and don’t want to lose it” problem.

### 2. Creator profiles

A home page for a person:

- bio
    
- avatar
    
- featured works
    
- collections
    
- artifact grid / shelves
    
- links out
    

This solves identity and return value.

### 3. Collections / exhibits

Users can group artifacts into:

- projects
    
- series
    
- moodboards
    
- research folders
    
- themed exhibitions
    

This matters because AI creation is rarely one-off. It is often iterative or thematic.

### 4. Discovery

Basic browse:

- newest
    
- trending
    
- underrated
    
- by type
    
- by tool
    
- by theme/tag
    

Not overbuilt recommendation. Just enough to make the site feel alive.

### 5. Social proof, light version

- likes
    
- saves
    
- comments
    
- reposts or remixes later
    

Not messaging yet. Messaging is almost never MVP.

## What makes this AI-native instead of just another portfolio site

This is the heart of the business.

The product should treat every artifact as having **metadata and genealogy**, not just a final file.

For each artifact, you may eventually want:

- prompt
    
- model used
    
- workflow/toolchain
    
- versions
    
- source material
    
- derivative/remix relations
    
- collaborators
    
- “inspired by”
    
- “forked from”
    
- seed/settings
    
- export formats
    
- attached notes / rationale
    

That is where a Git-like layer becomes powerful.

Not literally Git UX.  
But conceptually:

- original
    
- branch
    
- remix
    
- version history
    
- attribution chain
    

This could become a real moat if done elegantly.

Because AI creation is deeply iterative, and current platforms flatten that into “here’s an image.”

## The biggest strategic decision: file cabinet or social network?

This idea can go in two very different directions.

### Direction A: Personal vault first

“Save, organize, and showcase what you made.”

Benefits:

- easier onboarding
    
- real utility even with low network effects
    
- less cold-start risk
    
- can monetize earlier
    
- creator portfolio and archive are strong use cases
    

This is my preferred starting point.

### Direction B: Community feed first

“Share your AI stuff and discover others.”

Benefits:

- potentially faster growth if it hits
    
- more fun and alive
    
- stronger network effects
    

Risks:

- cold start
    
- moderation nightmare
    
- feed quality problem
    
- lots of noise, little retention
    

The graveyard is full of feed-first products with no enduring utility.

So I would start with **vault + profile + collections**, then add public discovery once there is enough inventory.

### 1. Casual makers

They made cool images, songs, stories, ideas, and want somewhere to keep/show them.

### 2. Power users / prompt artisans

They produce lots of artifacts and care about workflow, lineage, and audience.

### 3. Indie hackers / builders

They use AI to create tools, docs, demos, microproducts, datasets.

They produce white papers, position pieces, visual explainers, notebook outputs.

They want identity and following around AI-native output.

### 6. Curators

They may make less, but they want to collect, organize, exhibit, and contextualize.

The strongest early adopters are probably **power users + pseudonymous creators + curators**.

Not everybody.

## Why this might actually matter

There are a few deep reasons this could be important beyond novelty.

### 1. AI massively lowers creation cost

When production explodes, **curation, memory, identity, and filtering** become the scarce goods.

### 2. AI artifacts are born digital and highly remixable

That favors systems with versioning, attribution, and branching.

### 3. People want a “there” for what they’ve made

Especially when current creation tools are chat-based and ephemeral.

### 4. Multi-format creative identity is becoming normal

A single person can now make essays, tools, images, music, datasets, videos, and research artifacts. Existing platforms force fragmentation.

That last one is big. The product could become a kind of **multi-modal portfolio layer for the AI era**.

---

## Why it could fail

We should be blunt.

### 1. “AI slop” reputation

If quality control is weak, the site becomes a landfill very quickly.

### 2. Oversupply kills discovery

If everything can be created instantly, why should anyone browse?

### 3. Too many file types

Images, music, papers, videos, code, datasets, books, lists, etc. can make the product incoherent.

### 4. Provenance is messy

Users won’t always want to disclose prompts, tools, or source material.

### 5. Legal/moderation headaches

Copyright, impersonation, NSFW, spam, deepfakes, plagiarism, model abuse.

### 6. The feed trap

If you become feed-first, you may optimize for junk engagement instead of meaningful curation.

These are real.

---

## The critical product principle: quality over volume

This product dies if it becomes infinite sludge.

So one strategic principle should be:

**The site is for artifacts people consider worth keeping.**

That suggests product choices like:

- deliberate publishing, not auto-dumping
    
- curation tools
    
- collections
    
- featured works
    
- limited daily posts, maybe
    
- stronger profiles
    
- contextual metadata
    
- thoughtful discovery surfaces
    

In other words: **exhibition over stream**.

That feels much healthier.

## A cleaner MVP framing

Here is the version I would pitch:

### MVP concept

A simple web app where users can save, organize, and publicly showcase AI-created artifacts across formats.

### MVP jobs to be done

- “I made something cool and don’t want to lose it.”
    
- “I want a clean public page for my best AI-made work.”
    
- “I want to organize my creations into projects or collections.”
    
- “I want to browse interesting AI-made things without drowning in social media noise.”
    

### MVP object types

Do not support everything at first.

I would choose **3 artifact types max**:

- images
    
- text/docs
    
- audio
    

Or maybe:

- images
    
- writing
    
- projects
    

Video can come later. Datasets later. Books later. Databases later.

Scope discipline matters.

## A possible initial taxonomy

You need a strong object model early.

Each artifact could have:

- **Format**: image, essay, song, zine, dataset, project
    
- **Intent**: art, research, utility, story, argument, experiment
    
- **Tooling**: ChatGPT, Claude, Midjourney, Runway, Suno, Cursor, etc.
    
- **Visibility**: private, unlisted, public
    
- **Structure**: standalone, part of collection, remix, version
    

This helps discovery without overcomplication.
